# CPG Cranberry — Unbiased Open-Ended Interview Script (Step 2 / DE Step 20)

> **HUMAN VALIDATION MANDATE:** This script is first run against synthetic respondents to stress-test wording. Synthetic answers cannot predict real purchasing behavior, taste, or willingness-to-pay. The script's real purpose is to be used with REAL humans.

## Key Assumptions Under Test (ranked riskiest → least risky)

| Rank | ID | Assumption | Why it's risky |
|------|----|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | A3 — HABITUAL FREQUENCY | The product can establish a recurring everyday snacking habit, not a one-time novelty or seasonal gift. | LTV and the "Graza of cranberries" thesis both depend on repeat purchases. Novelty/gifting-only means a seasonal business. |
| 2 | A1 — CRAVEABILITY | Consumers desire an elevated, treat-like cranberry snack for personal consumption outside holiday/Thanksgiving occasions. | Cranberry has strong seasonal and "bitter/sauce" associations; without craving there's no trial. |
| 3 | A2 — WILLINGNESS-TO-PAY | Buyers will pay $9–$16 per package or $24 per 3-pack (3x–4x over $3–$4 Craisins). | The premium must be justified against luxury chocolate and candy, not just against Craisins. |
| 4 | A4 — POSITIONING & SOURCING | Luxury branding, clean label, and unique sourcing (white cranberries, artisanal prep) create genuine perceived value. | Could be "nice to have" rather than a purchase driver. |

## Interviewer Rules
1. Do **not** mention cranberries, CPG Cranberry, Graza, or any product concept before Section D.
2. No yes/no questions. No adjectives in questions that signal a desired answer ("delicious," "premium," "healthy").
3. Ask about **past behavior** ("tell me about the last time…") rather than hypotheticals.
4. Follow each answer with neutral probes: *"Tell me more." / "Why was that?" / "What happened next?" / "Can you give me an example?"*
5. Accept silence. Don't fill pauses with suggestions.
6. Record verbatim language, especially emotional words and brand names.

---

## Section A — Warm-Up (2 questions)

**Q1.** Walk me through your snacking on a typical weekday, from morning to night. What do you reach for, and when?

**Q2.** Think about the last time a snack left you disappointed or unsatisfied. What happened?

## Section B — Current Behavior & Alternatives (probing A3 and A1)

**Q3.** Tell me about the last snack you bought for yourself that felt like a treat. Where were you, what was it, and what made you pick it up? *(A1)*

**Q4.** Which snacks have you bought over and over for months? What keeps them in your rotation? *(A3)*

**Q5.** Tell me about a snack you were excited to try and then stopped buying. What changed? *(A3 — novelty decay)*

**Q6.** When you want something sweet in the afternoon or after dinner, what are the options you consider, and how do you choose between them? *(A1 — competitive set)*

**Q7.** How does fruit, in any form (fresh, dried, freeze-dried or in chocolate), fit into your snacking today? *(A1 — category entry point, unprompted)*

**Q8.** What comes to mind when you think about tart or sour flavors in snacks? Tell me about any you've enjoyed or avoided. *(A1 — flavor readiness, before naming cranberry)*

## Section C — Values, Price & Signals (probing A2 and A4)

**Q9.** Tell me about the most you've spent on a single snack or pantry item recently. What made it feel worth it, or not? *(A2 — price anchors)*

**Q10.** When you're standing in a store looking at two similar snacks at different prices, what do you look at to decide? *(A4 / A2 — value cues)*

**Q11.** How do the ingredients, where something comes from, or how it's made figure into what you buy? Give me a recent example. *(A4)*

**Q12.** Tell me about the last food or snack you gave as a gift or brought to someone's home. How did you choose it? *(A3 — gifting vs. self; DMU)*

## Section D — Concept Exposure (neutral description, read verbatim)

> "One idea some people are exploring is a line of cranberry snacks made to be eaten as an everyday treat, not just at the holidays. For example, they could use sweet white cranberries or be prepared by hand in small batches, with a short ingredient list and design-forward packaging. I'd like your honest reaction, positive or negative."

**Q13.** What's your first reaction to that? What thoughts come up? *(A1)*

**Q14.** When, if ever, can you picture yourself eating something like that? Walk me through the moment. *(A3 / A1)*

**Q15.** Which parts of that description matter to you, and which don't matter at all? *(A4)*

## Section E — Closing: Willingness-to-Pay & Trade-Offs

**Q16.** At what price would a package of this feel so cheap you'd doubt the quality? At what price would it start to feel expensive but still worth considering? At what price would it be too expensive to consider? *(A2 — Van Westendorp)*

**Q17.** If this were in front of you at checkout next to your current go-to treat, how would you decide between them? What would you give up to buy it? *(A2 — trade-off)*

**Q18.** How would you think about buying a single package versus a bundle of three? Who, if anyone, would you buy each one for? *(A2 / A3 — bundle, gift vs. self)*

**Q19.** What would have to be true for this to show up in your kitchen every week? *(A3)*

**Q20.** What should I have asked you that I didn't? *(open catch-all)*

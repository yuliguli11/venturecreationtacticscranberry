**Hannah:** Fresh fruit is a big part of it. Apples mostly, because they survive my bag, and clementines in winter. I'll do Trader Joe's freeze-dried strawberries sometimes, those are fun. Chocolate-covered fruit is a treat thing, like if it's around at a holiday party I'll eat it, but I don't buy it.

**Interviewer (probe):** Can you give me an example of the dried fruit being candy?

**Hannah:** Mango slices. I bought a bag of the sweetened ones once and looked at the label after I'd eaten half of it, and the sugar number was kind of shocking. Now I buy the unsweetened TJ's ones or I just skip it.

**Hannah:** I actually like tart a lot. Green apple, yuzu anything, I'll pick the sour option every time. There's a yuzu candy I get at H Mart that I love. What I don't like is when it's fake sour, like Sour Patch type, where it's sugar and citric acid and it burns your tongue. Real fruit tartness, yes. Neon sour candy, no.

**Hannah:** For food, probably a nice olive oil a friend recommended, like eighteen dollars, and I still kind of flinch thinking about it. And I say this as someone who spent a hundred and twenty dollars on a serum last month without blinking. But the serum works, I can see it on my skin. A snack is gone in ten minutes. Food is where I save so I can spend on my face.

**Interviewer (probe):** Why does the serum feel different?

**Hannah:** Evidence, basically. There's actual data behind the actives, and I use it for three months. With a fancy snack I'm paying for packaging and a story, and I'm very aware of that because I see the same trick in skincare marketing all day.

**Interviewer (Q10):** When you're standing in a store looking at two similar snacks at different prices, what do you look at to decide?

**Hannah:** First the price per ounce, honestly, I'm that person. Then I flip it over and read the ingredients and the sugar. If it's double or triple and it's the same thing in a nicer bag, I feel silly buying it. Words on the front like "superfood" actually make me trust it less.

**Interviewer (Q11):** How do the ingredients, where something comes from, or how it's made figure into what you buy? Give me a recent example.

**Hannah:** Ingredients matter a lot, sourcing and process mostly don't. Like, I switched to Larabars because the label is literally three things, dates and nuts. I don't really care if something was made in small batches or by hand, that doesn't change what's in it. "Handcrafted" is a marketing word to me. If you tell me the sugar is lower or it's just fruit, that's what I'll actually notice.

**Interviewer (Q1):** Walk me through your snacking on a typical weekday, from morning to night. What do you reach for, and when?

**Olivia:** Honestly, it's pretty planned. I'm at a 6am reformer class five days a week, then a protein matcha, and I don't really snack until mid-morning. Around eleven it's usually a Chomps stick, because I always have one in my bag when I'm out showing units. Afternoon is Greek yogurt with berries if I'm at the office, and after dinner maybe two squares of Hu or a Lily's bar. That's about it, two snacks a day, and I don't love grazing.

**Interviewer (Q2):** Think about the last time a snack left you disappointed or unsatisfied. What happened?

**Olivia:** A few weeks ago I grabbed a "protein" granola bar at a coffee shop because I'd forgotten my Chomps. I didn't flip it over until I'd eaten half of it, and it had something like fourteen grams of sugar and canola oil. I crashed hard around three and felt foggy in a client meeting. I was more annoyed at myself than the bar, honestly.

**Interviewer:** Why at yourself?

**Olivia:** Because I know better. I read labels on everything, and the one time I don't, that's what happens.

**Interviewer (Q3):** Tell me about the last snack you bought for yourself that felt like a treat. Where were you, what was it, and what made you pick it up?

**Olivia:** It was at Whole Foods on a Sunday, I picked up a Hu hazelnut butter bar. I'd seen someone I follow put it in a "what I eat in a day" video. It's not cheap, but the ingredient list is short, no refined sugar, no weird emulsifiers. It felt indulgent without the guilt spiral, and it looked nice in my tote, which, yes, I care about.

**Interviewer (Q4):** Which snacks have you bought over and over for months? What keeps them in your rotation?

**Olivia:** Chomps, for sure, I buy them on Thrive Market in bulk. Hu and Lily's, and Siete chips when I'm hosting. Magic Spoon on and off. What keeps them there is that I've already vetted the label and I don't have to think about it. They're boring in the best way, I know exactly what they do to my body.

**Interviewer (Q5):** Tell me about a snack you were excited to try and then stopped buying. What changed?

**Olivia:** There was a keto cookie brand I saw all over Instagram last year, I don't even remember the name now. The first box was fun, but they tasted kind of chalky and gave me a stomachache from the sugar alcohols. I bought maybe two boxes and then just drifted back to Hu. That happens a lot with me, actually. I'll try the hyped thing, and it's rare that it actually replaces something I already trust.

**Interviewer (Q6):** When you want something sweet in the afternoon or after dinner, what are the options you consider, and how do you choose between them?

**Olivia:** Dark chocolate is my default, like Hu or a Lily's bar, a couple squares. Sometimes frozen berries with a little yogurt, or a date with almond butter if I'm being "good." I choose based on what I've already eaten that day, if I've had carbs I'll skip the dates. Chocolate usually wins because it actually feels like dessert.

**Interviewer (Q7):** How does fruit, in any form (fresh, dried, freeze-dried or in chocolate), fit into your snacking today?

**Olivia:** Fresh berries every day, that's non-negotiable. Freeze-dried strawberries from Trader Joe's are something I keep around because they're just fruit, nothing added. Dried fruit I'm really wary of. I once bought a bag of "healthy" dried cranberries at Whole Foods thinking it was a good swap, flipped it over, and it was like 29 grams of sugar a serving. I actually posted a story about it, like, this is candy with a wellness font.

**Interviewer:** What happened next?

**Olivia:** I threw half the bag out and basically stopped buying dried fruit unless it says no sugar added in big letters. A lot of people DMed me saying they had no idea.

**Interviewer (Q8):** What comes to mind when you think about tart or sour flavors in snacks? Tell me about any you've enjoyed or avoided.

**Olivia:** I actually like tart. Green apple, raspberries, lemon in everything, those sour cherry things if they're unsweetened. What I avoid is sour candy, the stuff that's tart because it's coated in sugar and citric acid. Tart on its own feels clean to me; tart plus sugar feels like a trick.

**Interviewer (Q9):** Tell me about the most you've spent on a single snack or pantry item recently. What made it feel worth it, or not?

**Olivia:** Probably a $19 smoothie at a local Erewhon-style place in Brickell, and I'd do it again, because it's all organic and has collagen and I'm treating it like a meal. For a pantry item, a jar of raw honey for around $24. The smoothie felt worth it. A $12 bag of grain-free granola I bought once did not, because it was mostly coconut sugar and it was gone in two days.

**Interviewer (Q10):** When you're standing in a store looking at two similar snacks at different prices, what do you look at to decide?

**Olivia:** I flip them both over before I even look at the price. Added sugar, what oils are in there, how many ingredients. If the more expensive one has a cleaner label, I buy it, no question. If the more expensive one is just prettier and has the same sugar, I get genuinely irritated, that's where I feel scammed.

**Interviewer (Q11):** How do the ingredients, where something comes from, or how it's made figure into what you buy? Give me a recent example.

**Olivia:** Ingredients are almost everything. Where it comes from matters less unless it changes the ingredients, like grass-fed for Chomps. How it's made, like "small batch," honestly I tune that out, every brand says it. Recent example, I put back a pretty almond butter cup because it had sunflower oil, even though the packaging was gorgeous.

**Interviewer (Q12):** Tell me about the last food or snack you gave as a gift or brought to someone's home. How did you choose it?

**Olivia:** I brought a box of Compartés chocolate to a coworker's birthday brunch. I chose it because it looked beautiful and felt special, and I figured she wasn't going to read the label like I do. For gifts I care more about how it looks and less about the macros, which, yeah, is a little hypocritical. I gift maybe five times a year, so it's not a big category for me.

**Interviewer:** "One idea some people are exploring is a line of cranberry snacks made to be eaten as an everyday treat, not just at the holidays. For example, they could use sweet white cranberries or be prepared by hand in small batches, with a short ingredient list and design-forward packaging. I'd like your honest reaction, positive or negative."

**Interviewer (Q13):** What's your first reaction to that? What thoughts come up?

**Olivia:** My first thought is, how much sugar. Every dried cranberry I've ever seen is basically soaked in sugar, because cranberries are too sour to eat on their own. So "sweet white cranberries" makes me a little suspicious, is that sweet naturally or sweet because you added stuff? If the label were genuinely clean I'd be curious, but right now it sounds like it could be fancy Craisins.

**Interviewer:** Tell me more.

**Olivia:** Cranberry also just reads as Thanksgiving to me, or juice for a UTI. It's not something I crave. I'd need to see the back of the package before I felt anything either way.

**Interviewer (Q14):** When, if ever, can you picture yourself eating something like that? Walk me through the moment.

**Olivia:** Maybe the afternoon slot, if it replaced the yogurt on a day I'm out showing properties and need something in my bag. Or tossed on yogurt instead of berries. But I'm not going to lie, it'd have to beat Chomps or Hu, and that's a high bar. I can more easily picture putting it on a brunch board once than eating it every Tuesday.

**Interviewer (Q15):** Which parts of that description matter to you, and which don't matter at all?

**Olivia:** Short ingredient list matters the most, that's the only reason I'd pick it up. Packaging matters a bit, I like minimal and neutral, not cutesy, and I want the sugar number called out on the front if it's low. "By hand in small batches" doesn't matter to me at all, it doesn't tell me anything about what's in it. White cranberries are interesting, but only if that means less added sugar.

**Interviewer (Q16):** At what price would a package of this feel so cheap you'd doubt the quality? At what price would it start to feel expensive but still worth considering? At what price would it be too expensive to consider?

**Olivia:** Under about $4 I'd assume it's the same sugary stuff in a nicer bag. Around $9 or $10 it feels expensive but I'd consider it, that's Hu bar territory times two. At $15 I'm out, because at that point I'm comparing it to a smoothie or a really good chocolate bar. And to be clear, if the sugar is high, it's too expensive at any price.

**Interviewer:** Why is the sugar tied to price for you?

**Olivia:** Because paying extra for clean feels like an investment, paying extra for sugar feels like being played.

**Interviewer (Q17):** If this were in front of you at checkout next to your current go-to treat, how would you decide between them? What would you give up to buy it?

**Olivia:** I'd flip both over, same as always. If it had less added sugar than the Hu bar and it was just fruit, I might swap it in once to try it. I wouldn't give up the Chomps, that's function, not a treat. Realistically I'd probably grab the Hu, because I already know it and chocolate is more satisfying.

**Interviewer (Q18):** How would you think about buying a single package versus a bundle of three? Who, if anyone, would you buy each one for?

**Olivia:** Single for me, to test it. I'd never buy three of something I haven't tried, my pantry is full of stuff I didn't finish. A three-pack I could see as a hostess gift if the packaging is beautiful, maybe up to $25 for all three, but then I'm competing with Compartés. For myself, three would only happen after I'd bought the single more than once.

**Interviewer (Q19):** What would have to be true for this to show up in your kitchen every week?

**Olivia:** Honestly, a lot. Low or no added sugar, like under five grams, no seed oils, and it has to taste good without the sugar crutch. Probably someone I trust, like a wellness creator, eating it regularly and not just in a sponsored post. And it'd need to be at Whole Foods or on Thrive, I'm not ordering a special snack from a website. Even then, my track record says I try new things and go back to my staples, so weekly is a stretch.

**Interviewer (Q20):** What should I have asked you that I didn't?

**Olivia:** You didn't ask about blood sugar, and for people like me that's the whole thing. I'd want to know if it spikes you, like, has anyone put it on a CGM. If you could say "doesn't spike you" and prove it, that would honestly matter more to me than the packaging or the white cranberries.

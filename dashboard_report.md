# CPG Cranberry: Synthetic Primary Market Research Synthesis Report

Study: Beachhead test of a luxury "Graza-of-cranberries" snack brand for the chic Happier Grocery / Erewhon shopper
Sample: 10 synthetic respondents (9 aesthetic-driven women in the luxury-grocery orbit, plus 1 household economic buyer)
Instrument: Unbiased open-ended interview script, Q1–Q20, testing assumptions A1–A4
Companion data file: synthesis_data.json

## HUMAN VALIDATION MANDATE

> **WARNING: EVERY FINDING IN THIS REPORT IS A HYPOTHESIS, NOT EVIDENCE.**
>
> All 10 respondents are synthetic, AI-generated personas answering an AI-run interview. They cannot predict real purchasing behavior, taste, or willingness-to-pay. The numbers below are counts of what synthetic characters said, not measurements of a market. No pricing, product, channel, or fundraising decision should rest on this report. Each finding must be confirmed or killed through real human interviews, blind taste tests, smoke tests, pre-orders, and in-store sales (for example a Happier Grocery pop-up). Section 15 sets out that plan.
>
> Treat this report as a way to sharpen questions. It does not answer them.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Habit (A3) is Invalidated.** 0 of 10 respondents support a recurring everyday habit, and 9 of 10 (90%) contradict it. No respondent said they would buy monthly or weekly. Stated behavior was 7 "Try once" (70%) and 3 "Occasional gift" (30%). The "Graza of cranberries" repeat-purchase thesis gets no support from this round.
- **Craveability (A1) is only weakly Partially Validated.** No respondent expressed unconditional craving (0%). Six (60%) could picture a personal non-holiday moment, and every one of those was conditional on taste, low sugar, or format. Four (40%) could not picture it at all. Chocolate is the incumbent treat for 9 of 10.
- **WTP (A2) is Partially Validated, and only at the bottom of the band or for gifts.** The median self-purchase "expensive-but-considering" price is **$8.00** (mean $8.50), below the $9 floor. Only 4 of 10 would consider $9 or more for themselves. For 8 of 10, the self-purchase "too expensive" threshold is $16 or less (median $12.50). Five of 10 would pay $24 or more for a 3-pack, but **all five only as a gift or hosting set. None would pay that for their own consumption.**
- **Positioning and sourcing (A4) are Partially Validated.** They drive trial and gifting, not repeat purchase or a premium on self-purchase. Short ingredient list (8 of 10) and low sugar (8 of 10) are the table-stakes gates. White cranberry is the only element that created curiosity (9 of 10 called it new or interesting). "Small batch / handmade" was dismissed by 9 of 10.
- **"Fancy Craisins" is the category frame.** All 10 respondents said it unprompted at first reaction, and all 10 raised Thanksgiving. The concept is read as dried fruit with sugar in a nicer bag until the product proves otherwise.
- **Biggest unmet need (hypothesis):** an indulgent but genuinely low-sugar afternoon treat that doesn't cause a crash, isn't a "chalk/sand" letdown, and is tart-forward and portable. Runner-up signals are a non-generic hostess-gift tin (the "anti-Godiva"), a tart/salty-spiced flavor profile, and cheese-board pairing.
- **Strategic read:** the evidence points toward a gift- and trial-led entry, a lower self-purchase price, a tart-forward low-sugar product, and sampling-led distribution, with habit treated as unproven until real repeat data exists.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Respondent profiles
Ten synthetic end-user profiles (v2) sit inside the founder's beachhead: primarily women aged 25–41 in luxury-grocery metros (NYC core; LA, Miami and Austin secondary), with individual income of $110k–$140k or household income of $140k–$190k. All shop at least monthly at Happier Grocery, Erewhon, Whole Foods, Citarella, or similar. The skeptics were drawn from inside that world on purpose. Daniel Chen is retained as the household economic buyer and gifter, to inform the decision-making unit (DMU).

### 2.3 Interview script
There were 20 open-ended questions in five sections. A is warm-up (Q1–Q2). B covers current behavior and alternatives (Q3–Q8, probing A1 and A3). C covers values, price and signals (Q9–Q12, probing A2 and A4). D is a neutral concept statement read verbatim, followed by reactions (Q13–Q15). E covers the Van Westendorp price questions, trade-offs, bundles and habit (Q16–Q20).

### 2.4 Anti-bias guardrails (from the script)
1. Cranberries, the brand, Graza and the concept were not mentioned before Section D.
2. No yes/no questions and no leading adjectives ("delicious", "premium", "healthy").
3. Questions asked about past behavior ("tell me about the last time…") rather than hypotheticals.
4. Only neutral probes were used ("Tell me more", "Why was that?", "What happened next?").
5. Silence was accepted and pauses were not filled.
6. Verbatim language was recorded.

### 2.5 Coding rules used in this synthesis
- **Supports:** the respondent expresses the assumed attitude or behavior without major conditions.
- **Partial:** the respondent expresses it only conditionally or in a limited context (for example gift only, only if low sugar, only once).
- **Contradicts:** the respondent explicitly rejects it or cannot picture it.
- **Assumption status:** Validated means 60% or more Supports. Invalidated means 20% or less Supports **and** a majority (over 50%) Contradicts. Anything else is Partially Validated.
- **Prices:** ranges are coded at their midpoint ($11–12 = $11.50, $28–30 = $29, $18–20 = $19, $12–13 = $12.50, $9–10 = $9.50, "above $10–11" = $10.50, "$8–9" = $8.50). "Under $X" is coded as X. Daniel's 3-pack figure of "under $30" is coded as $30, an upper bound. "Would not buy" is excluded from the 3-pack averages and reported separately.
- **Objections:** counts come from the full transcript, not only the summary block.

### 2.6 Limitations of synthetic research (read before using any number)
- **n = 10, synthetic.** One respondent equals 10 percentage points, so no percentage here is statistically meaningful.
- **Uniform language is a warning sign.** All 10 said "fancy Craisins", all 10 said "Thanksgiving", and all 10 described a chalk- or sand-like "healthy snack" disappointment at Q2. Real people are rarely this aligned. The model producing the respondents probably converges on the same framings, which makes consensus look stronger than it is and hides real variance.
- **No taste stimulus.** Respondents reacted to a sentence, not a product. Craveability and repeat purchase can't be measured without tasting.
- **Stated vs. revealed behavior.** Van Westendorp answers and stated frequency are known to diverge from real purchases, and synthetic answers add a second layer of that gap.
- **Profile design bias.** The profiles were written to match the founder's hypothesis of a luxury beachhead with skeptics drawn from inside it. Different profiles would produce different results.
- **Same model family.** The interviewer, the respondents and this analyst are all AI, so shared blind spots are likely.

## 11. Segment Differences

### 11.1 Innovators (Sienna, Chloe)
- They have the highest WTP (self EC $11.50, TE $18). They are the only two coded Supports on A2, and in both cases for a single trial.
- They buy for novelty and content ("my novelty budget", "buy it once for the picture"). Both warn against building on them ("Don't trust people like me too much").
- Chloe values packaging and story most. Sienna wants a short label, low sugar, tart flavor, and a named-farm story.

### 11.2 Early Adopters (Maya, Priya)
- Maya (the Step 5 persona) is surprisingly price-sensitive for herself (EC $8, TE $13). She is the clearest voice for a salty-tart, li-hing-mui-style profile and for weekly-store availability.
- Priya is the clearest example of a split between self and gift. As a self-buyer her ceiling is $8–9. As a gifter she'd pay $18–28 for a single and $40 for three separable tins. She calls sweet white cranberry "solving the wrong problem".

### 11.3 Early Majority (Zoe, Olivia, Hannah, Daniel)
- Mean self EC is $7.13. Each has a different gate: virality and sour candy (Zoe), sugar and blood-sugar proof (Olivia), sampling and per-ounce value (Hannah), and spouse approval plus price per ounce (Daniel).
- The group converges on mass or weekly channels (H-E-B, Target, Whole Foods, Thrive, TJ's, Fairway, Costco).

### 11.4 Late Majority and Skeptic (Rachel, Lauren)
- Both are chocolate loyalists, strongly tied to Thanksgiving (both host it), and would at most try it once.
- Rachel's decisions are gated by her kids ("if they won't eat it, it doesn't matter what I think").
- Lauren rejects "cute" design and social discovery. She trusts shopkeepers and provenance, and sees a possible opening only on a cheese board, which "is putting it right back into Thanksgiving".

### 11.5 Self vs. gift
- Stated WTP roughly doubles in gift contexts: Priya goes from $8–9 to $18 EC, and Daniel from $6 to $15 EC.
- 100% of 3-pack ceilings at $24 or more are gift or hosting contexts.
- Seasonality pulls hardest on the gift frame. Maya, Priya, Rachel and Lauren all tie cranberry gifting to November–December ("If I bring it in June they'll wonder why I brought cranberries", Priya).

### 11.6 Daniel as economic buyer (DMU)
- Discovery happens through his wife (Instagram screenshots and his gift album). Recurring purchases go through his spreadsheet ("I'd be the one approving it, and I cancel things that pile up").
- He runs two budgets: groceries, which he optimizes per ounce ($9 is his self ceiling), and gifts for his wife, which he doesn't optimize (up to about $20).
- Implication: a subscription or "everyday" product has to pass two gates in couple households, taste (the partner) and value per ounce (the payer). A gift product only has to pass the partner's taste, through the wishlist.

## 12. Biggest Unmet Need (hypothesis)

### An indulgent, genuinely low-sugar "afternoon slump" treat: real-fruit tartness, no crash, portable

The most widespread unmet need in these transcripts is not "a luxury cranberry snack". It is a treat for the weekday afternoon slot and away-from-home moments that feels indulgent but doesn't bring a sugar crash, bloating, or a chalk-and-sand health-food letdown. Cranberry can compete for this slot only if the product is tart-forward, verifiably low in added sugar, and portable or resealable. The current "sweet white cranberry, design-forward" concept doesn't yet show any of these.

Supporting evidence:
- **10 of 10** recalled a recent disappointment with a snack that over-promised (8 were "protein" or "healthy" snacks). They described them as tasting of chalk, sand, drywall, pencil eraser, or candle wax.
- **8 of 10** described a defined afternoon snack moment. Sienna: "It's honestly the most important snack of my day." Zoe and Hannah both call it "the danger zone".
- Current away-from-home options disappoint: Maya's office snack wall ("meh"), Priya's hotel minibar ("just so depressing"), Hannah's break-room pastries, and Olivia's crash after a coffee-shop bar.
- **8 of 10** raised sugar concerns. Sienna: "No sugar crash, no bloating, no guilt spiral… That's really the bar for anything that becomes a daily thing for me."
- **7 of 10** like tart flavors unprompted but reject candy-sour ("sugar with acid"). **6 of 10** could picture the concept in a personal afternoon or desk slot, and every one of them was conditional.

Caveat: incumbents such as Hu, dates, TJ's mango, Chomps and Larabar already serve this slot well enough for many respondents. The need is real, but the bar to replace an incumbent is high ("it has to be better, not just newer", Sienna).

### Runner-up opportunity signals
1. **A non-generic hostess or small-gift tin (the "anti-Godiva").** This is the only context where price clears $9 for Priya and Daniel, and where 5 respondents go to $24 or more for a trio. Priya: "I refuse to show up with Godiva." Daniel: "The worst outcome is spending $40 on something that looks like I grabbed it at a CVS." *Risk:* it pulls the brand toward November–December seasonality.
2. **A tart, sour or salty-spiced flavor profile** (li hing mui, amla, chili, yuzu, sour-coated). Maya: "If it tasted like a sour plum kind of thing, salty-tart, that would get my attention way more than "white cranberries."" Priya wants "a tart version, maybe with chili". Zoe would consider it at 3pm only as "a sour-coated cranberry thing". The counter-signal is Rachel (her kids) and Lauren, who want it sweeter and less bitter.
3. **Cheese-board and entertaining pairing.** For 8 of 10, the first or easiest moment to imagine was a board or party. Lauren: "tart fruit makes sense next to something rich and salty." This points to cheesemonger-counter sampling. *Risk:* hosting is occasional, and Priya reports dried fruit left over from boards going to waste.
4. **A verified low-sugar or "doesn't spike you" tart treat.** Olivia: "If you could say "doesn't spike you" and prove it, that would honestly matter more to me than the packaging or the white cranberries."
